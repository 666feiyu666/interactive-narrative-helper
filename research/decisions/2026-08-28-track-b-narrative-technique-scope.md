# Decision: Track B narrative-technique scope

- **Date:** 2026-08-28
- **Status:** confirmed by the user in project discussion
- **Scope:** Track B research identity, research-question hierarchy, component
  ownership, and program-framework representation
- **Supersedes:** the parts of
  [`2026-08-28-two-track-helper-architecture.md`](2026-08-28-two-track-helper-architecture.md)
  and
  [`2026-08-28-initial-helper-development.md`](2026-08-28-initial-helper-development.md)
  that identify all of Track B with the Counterfactual Narrative Design Partner

## Decision

Track B is the umbrella program's **story-level narrative technique
exploration** direction. Its prospective design-time artifact is the
author-controlled **Story-level Narrative Technique Design Partner**.

Track B may frame inspectable, technique-specific transformations of a
concrete story. These transformations may concern events and consequences,
perspective and information, time and sequence, or role and agency. These are
non-exhaustive dimensions of the conceptual space, not a list of implemented
modules or confirmed future experiments.

The existing **Counterfactual Narrative Design Partner** is retained as
**Track B1 — Counterfactual Action and Consequence Exploration**. Its research
question, case, proposal contracts, capability dimensions, and author-control
boundary remain intact. It is the only currently specified Track B technique
module.

No generic narrative-technique case or proposal schema is introduced by this
decision. No perspective, focalization, temporal, information, role, or agency
module is created. A second technique probe is not required to correct the
program-level classification; evidence would be required before claiming that
an Agent can perform or share a valid contract across those techniques.

Track B remains outside the current development scope. This decision does not
authorize a runtime, Harness, prompt, provider integration, interface,
evaluation implementation, design-brief schema, or experiment.

## Research-question hierarchy

The new Track B program-level question is:

> To what extent can a generative agent act as an author-controlled,
> story-level narrative technique design partner in adapting classic
> children's stories into interactive narratives, by proposing inspectable
> technique-specific transformations and explaining their implications for
> player experience, interaction, narrative development, thematic meaning,
> and educational purpose?

The previous long-term counterfactual research question is preserved as the
Track B1 research question rather than rewritten as though the project had
always studied a general narrative-technique Agent.

## Context and design episode

### Prior state

The two-track architecture correctly separated macro educational-design
exploration from concrete story-level exploration, but it defined the whole
story-level track through one technique: changing a pivotal character action
and projecting alternative consequences.

### Triggering tension

Project discussion identified perspective or focalization as a valid
interactive-narrative operation that need not change the story's events or
endings. A player may revisit the same story from another character's position,
receive different information, or compare interpretations. This conceptual
counterexample shows that interactive narrative can transform experience,
knowledge, and interpretation as well as causal plot development.

### Interpretation

Counterfactual exploration is therefore not the superordinate definition of
story-level interactive-narrative design. It is a strong, bounded technique
that activates several separable capability dimensions: substantive action
difference, source compatibility, causal coherence, interactive potential,
meaning projection, and author control.

The current interpretation is a program-architecture judgment. It is not
evidence that a generative agent can use perspective, focalization, or any
other additional technique effectively.

## Alternatives considered

### Keep all of Track B counterfactual

This preserves the existing names and contracts but makes the program
framework narrower than the story-level interactive-narrative space it claims
to organize.

### Expand Track B into a generic story-writing Agent

This removes the technique mismatch but erases the design-time, inspectable,
author-controlled boundary and risks turning the research into autonomous full
story generation.

### Use a technique-family Track B with a focused B1 module — selected

This keeps a stable story-level program boundary while preserving the depth,
testability, and independent contribution of the counterfactual research.

## Consequences

- Track A remains the macro Educational Interactive Narrative Design Helper and
  is not changed by this decision.
- The human-reviewed design brief remains the conceptual bridge between macro
  direction and a concrete story task; it is still not a frozen schema.
- Track B's common documentation can describe a concrete story, reviewed
  design intention, author-declared invariants and permitted changes,
  technique framing, inspectable proposals, and author authority.
- Pivotal action, character goal, counterfactual question, alternative action,
  and causal consequence projection remain Track B1-specific concepts.
- Track B shared evaluation concerns may include source compatibility,
  substantive transformation, interactive potential, inspectability, meaning
  projection, and author utility and control. Each technique still requires
  its own operational criteria and evidence.
- The inactive Track B component boundary is renamed and nests the preserved
  counterfactual schemas under its only specified technique module.
- The full program framework figure represents story-level technique space
  without presenting example techniques as implemented capabilities or
  progress states.

## Claim boundary

This decision supports the claim that the program's conceptual story-level
space is broader than counterfactual branching. It does not support claims that
the prospective Partner is technique-general, that the listed techniques share
a valid machine-readable contract, or that any Track B capability is
implemented or effective.

## Reversibility

The exact Track B artifact name, directory nesting, and future technique
contracts remain reversible. The durable change is that the story-level track
is organized as a human-controlled narrative-technique family while the
counterfactual task remains a separately specified, independently evaluable
research focus.
