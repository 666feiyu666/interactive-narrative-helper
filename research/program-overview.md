# Interactive Storytelling Research Program

This workspace groups research on interactive narrative, AI-mediated
storytelling, and the relationship between generative freedom and authored
rules.

## Current research stage

The active stage uses data mining to understand how Interactive Fiction is used
in educational and learning contexts. It currently focuses on terminology,
candidate coverage, source acquisition, inclusion criteria, and the kinds of
application and design information that can be supported by the evidence.

The findings may later support a design knowledge base, information retrieval,
and an educational-IF Agent. Their detailed contracts are deliberately deferred
until the mining results establish what knowledge is reliable and useful. Raw
HTML is source material for mining, not direct input to that future Agent.

## Long-term research direction

The program retains the confirmed long-term question of whether a generative
agent can act as a counterfactual narrative design partner when authors adapt
classic children's stories into multi-ending interactive narratives. That
future partner should help an author explore alternative character actions and
project consequences for plot, endings, theme, and educational meaning.

The current educational-IF mining stage does not silently redefine or claim to
implement that long-term artifact.

## Existing research instrument

The independently versioned `Fox-and-Crow` application is the first playable
testbed. It remains a research instrument and demonstrator rather than the
definition of the whole research program.

## Program boundary

The project distinguishes author-facing design-time assistance from runtime
story narration. General media-generation and interactive-runtime tools remain
outside this repository unless they are brought in through an explicit project
contract.
